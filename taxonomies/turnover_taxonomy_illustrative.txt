TAXONOMY OF CAUSES OF EMPLOYEE TURNOVER (IDs are important) — ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT VALIDATED
Built for the example figure of the OrgSci proposal; it has not been piloted or benchmarked against human coders.

Level: Job and Working Conditions
Definition: Narratives that attribute turnover to features of the job itself and to the conditions under which it is performed.

#1 Pay & Benefits
Turnover attributed to salaries, bonuses or benefits that are low or below what other employers offer.
Example: "People leave because they are underpaid."

#2 Workload & Working Hours
Turnover attributed to excessive workload, long hours, overtime or work intensity.
Example: "Everyone is working sixty-hour weeks."

#3 Staffing Shortages
Turnover attributed to the unit being understaffed: unfilled positions, hiring freezes, too few people for the work.
Example: "We have had three vacancies for months."

#4 Burnout & Job Stress
Turnover attributed to exhaustion, burnout, chronic stress or strain experienced by employees.
Example: "People are simply exhausted."

#5 Work–Life Balance & Flexibility
Turnover attributed to the lack of flexible hours, remote work, or the conflict between work and private life.
Example: "We do not allow remote work, so parents leave."

Level: Management and Organization
Definition: Narratives that attribute turnover to how employees are managed and to features of the organization.

#6 Leadership & Supervision Quality
Turnover attributed to poor managers or supervisors: lack of support, unfair treatment, poor communication by the direct manager.
Example: "People don't leave companies, they leave bad bosses."

#7 Recognition & Feedback
Turnover attributed to the lack of recognition, appreciation or performance feedback.
Example: "Nobody ever tells them they did a good job."

#8 Career Development & Promotion Opportunities
Turnover attributed to limited promotion prospects, training, or career paths.
Example: "There is no way up here."

#9 Restructuring & Job Insecurity
Turnover attributed to reorganizations, layoffs, mergers, or fear of losing the job.
Example: "Since the merger everyone fears for their job."

#10 Team Climate & Organizational Culture
Turnover attributed to conflict among colleagues, a toxic or unwelcoming culture, or poor fit with organizational values.
Example: "The atmosphere in the team is poisonous."

Level: External Factors
Definition: Narratives that attribute turnover to conditions outside the organization.

#11 Outside Job Opportunities
Turnover attributed to a tight labour market, competitors poaching staff, or attractive offers elsewhere.
Example: "Competitors keep hiring our best engineers."
